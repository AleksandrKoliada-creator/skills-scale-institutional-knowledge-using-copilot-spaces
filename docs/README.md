# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository captures the project management practices used by OctoAcme to move work from idea to delivery. The overall approach is lightweight, iterative, and grounded in clear ownership. Teams begin with initiation to confirm the business problem, define success metrics, identify stakeholders, and decide whether the initiative should continue into planning. Once approved, work shifts into planning, where priorities are clarified, backlog items are estimated, dependencies are mapped, and milestones and release timing are aligned with the delivery team.

During execution, OctoAcme emphasizes small, testable increments supported by repeatable delivery routines. Daily standups and weekly project check-ins help teams surface blockers, coordinate dependencies, and keep progress visible. The project board is used to track work across stages such as Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done, while pull requests are expected to be small, linked to issues, and accompanied by acceptance criteria. Team roles are explicit: project managers coordinate schedules, risks, and communication; product managers define outcomes and prioritize value; developers implement and test the work; QA validates quality and acceptance; and stakeholders provide approvals and business context. This structure supports OctoAcme’s principles of customer-first delivery, iterative execution, measurable outcomes, and transparent ownership.

Communication is a core part of the process. The team uses regular standups, weekly PM and product alignment, stakeholder updates, and escalation paths for high-impact risks or blockers. Risk and dependency management are documented in a risk register, with mitigations tracked over time and reviewed in project syncs. For incidents and major releases, communications are expected to be timely and clear, with owners, expected timelines, and follow-up actions documented. These practices create a single source of truth for status and ensure that decisions, issues, and escalations are communicated consistently across engineering, product, and stakeholder groups.

Quality assurance is built into the process rather than treated as a final step. The docs recommend unit, integration, and smoke testing depending on the scope of the work, along with security scanning and CI validation before release. Definition of Done and acceptance criteria are used to confirm that work is ready to ship, and retrospectives help the team identify improvements, assign owners, and track follow-up actions. By combining disciplined planning, clear role ownership, proactive communication, and strong QA practices, OctoAcme creates a repeatable process for delivering value while reducing risk and learning from each project cycle.
